From kinematic to dynamic waves

Understand which physics are retained and how they are computed over cells and time steps. Compare the approaches, then open the topics relevant to your analysis.

Separate two choices

01 · Equations and the meaning of each term

Why shallow-water equations?

Shallow refers to depth relative to the horizontal variation length, not just a small absolute depth. Depth averaging and a hydrostatic approximation reduce the role of vertical acceleration. Deep rivers can meet these assumptions; strongly vertical jets and circulation need additional treatment.

∂A/∂t + ∂Q/∂x = ql

1D continuity: area A [m²], discharge Q [m³/s], lateral inflow per unit length qₗ [m²/s].

∂q/∂t + ∂(q²/h)/∂x + gh ∂h/∂x = gh(S0 − Sf)

Illustrative momentum equation for a simple constant-width channel, omitting lateral momentum and additional forces. q = hu is unit-width discharge [m²/s], u velocity, h depth, S₀ bed slope and S_f signed friction slope.

∂q/∂t
Local inertia: unit-width discharge changes over time at a location.
∂(q²/h)/∂x
Advective acceleration: water moves into a region with different velocity.
gh ∂h/∂x
Pressure gradient due to depth differences; together with bed slope it gives the water-surface gradient.

Kinematic flow uses S_f ≈ S₀. Diffusion wave retains the surface-gradient balance but drops accelerations. Local inertia restores temporal acceleration; dynamic wave also retains advection. Spatial dimension (1D/2D) is a separate choice.

Theory references: USACE · Kinematic wave · Clawpack · Shallow-water equations

03 · Computing fluxes and storage on a mesh

Finite volumes and finite differences

Finite volumes update a cell’s conserved amount from boundary fluxes. A shared interface flux cancels in the sum over neighboring cells. Finite differences approximate derivatives from neighboring values and can also be conservative; the names alone do not rank accuracy.

Vin+1 = Vin + Δt (Qin − Qout + Qsource)

Illustrative conservative update of volume V [m³] using discharge Q [m³/s]. Sum signed flows over all faces when a cell has multiple boundaries.

Mass conservation alone does not make momentum representations equivalent. Kinematic routing closes discharge with a storage relation; local inertia uses previous flow and surface gradients; Full SWE also updates momentum fluxes.

Resolution and numerical diffusion

Reconstruction and flux evaluation affect how strongly sharp changes are smeared. Higher-order reconstruction can improve smooth regions, while limiting is needed near abrupt changes. Continued change under refinement indicates that resolution convergence has not yet been established.

04 · Explicit, implicit and semi-implicit time stepping

Explicit methods evaluate updates from known states. Implicit methods include new-time unknowns and require a coupled or nonlinear solve. Semi-implicit methods mix treatments across terms. These names describe time evaluation, not different physical processes.

(yn+1 − yn)/Δt = f(yn)

Forward Euler: an illustrative explicit time step.

(yn+1 − yn)/Δt = f(yn+1)

Backward Euler: an illustrative implicit time step, not a claim that every K-DRUM mode uses this formula.

Implicit stepping can still smear peaks and timing with large steps, and nonlinear iterations must converge. Explicit stepping must respect propagation relative to cell size. Stable completion does not establish temporal accuracy.

C = (|u| + √(gh)) Δt / Δx

Illustrative Courant number for 1D shallow-water waves. Limits depend on the method; 2D geometry, sources and boundaries may impose additional restrictions.

Reduce the timestep and compare peak, arrival time and balance at key locations. Forcing intervals, internal steps and output intervals can differ; output timestamps do not reveal every internal step.

06 · Boundaries, wet/dry fronts and verification

Boundary conditions connect the model to its surroundings

Inflow discharge, downstream stage, tide, walls and open boundaries provide different information. Subcritical flow can transmit downstream-stage influence upstream; inflow alone does not determine stage. Boundary number and type must suit the flow regime and solver.

Wetting and drying require physically admissible storage

Excessive outflow from a shallow cell can produce negative depth. Available-water limits and wet/dry handling are needed; velocity near a depth threshold is sensitive because it divides discharge by small depth. Clipping depth alone is not a conservation check.

  1. Lake at rest: does varying terrain create artificial flow in a resting state?
  2. Conservation: do boundary fluxes, sources, losses and storage change balance?
  3. Propagation and fronts: how sensitive are arrival times and wetting fronts to grid and timestep?
  4. Observations: have stage, discharge and inundation been compared with independent data?

These are criteria for assessing a simulation, not a claim that every listed capability has passed every test.

Inputs and computation

How it works and its scope

Elevation adjustment changes the rainfall mapping, whereas QC inspects source-data quality. Read the two results separately to trace the cause of a change.

Understand the mechanism

Representing an elevation–rainfall relationship and screening observations are separate tasks. Treating missing values as zero implies dry weather; adjusting suspect values can spread their error spatially.

Rainfall completeness assessment

When checking gaps in the rainfall used for a run

What you gain

Determine whether the event or low-flow period includes missing or forecast inputs.

How it works · figure guide · checks

Reading the illustration

Inputs and computation

  1. Input / state — observed, forecast and missing periods
  2. Processing — duration totals by data category
  3. Output / use — forcing composition and gap summary

How it works and its scope

Durations are counted by observed, forecast and missing categories. Review both the overall composition and gaps within the period of interest when interpreting an event.

Understand the mechanism

Completeness describes whether required time intervals have usable input. Observations and forecasts can fill the same record while retaining different uncertainty; coverage is not an accuracy score.

Apply it to interpretation

A short gap before a flood peak may matter more for that event than a longer dry-period gap. Locate missing intervals on the event timeline instead of relying only on an overall percentage.

Checks when interpreting results

Check missing intervals and the forecast portion of the record. These counts do not fill missing rainfall.

Green-Ampt infiltration

When examining how rainfall infiltrates the soil

What you gain

Understand why soil conditions change the surface-runoff contribution under the same rainfall.

How it works · figure guide · checks

Reading the illustration

Distinguish rainfall at the surface, downward infiltration and wetting-front movement. Image labels are in Korean.

Inputs and computation

  1. Input / state — rain
  2. Processing — wetting front
  3. Output / use — rain excess

How it works and its scope

Rainfall intensity, initial moisture and permeability affect the division between infiltration and excess rainfall. Water entering the soil contributes to storage; the excess feeds surface runoff.

Understand the mechanism

Green–Ampt represents infiltration capacity through wetting-front moisture contrast, suction and conductivity. Actual infiltration is also limited by water supply and available soil storage; rainfall intensity relative to capacity governs surface excess.

Apply it to interpretation

Antecedent wetness can change the early response of the same soil. Before adjusting conductivity to reduce an excessive peak, examine initial moisture, impervious fraction and rainfall timing.

Checks when interpreting results

Review soil properties and initial moisture; infiltration and downstream discharge belong to different calculation stages.

Further explanation

The optional impervious-area split separates rainfall contributions using the paved fraction. Check that this option is enabled in the chosen path and review the land-cover data.

Surface & subsurface runoff

When tracing runoff over the surface and through the soil

What you gain

Separate rapid surface response from runoff contributed through soil layers.

How it works · figure guide · checks

Reading the illustration

Follow the surface and soil-layer pathways toward the channel. Image labels are in Korean.

Inputs and computation

  1. Input / state — soil storage
  2. Processing — surface/subsurface
  3. Output / use — river inflow

How it works and its scope

Surface and soil-layer flows travel at different rates depending on slope and storage. Once these cell contributions enter a channel, channel routing follows.

Understand the mechanism

Generation partitions rainfall into infiltration, storage and runoff; routing moves that water downstream. Rapid surface response and slower soil drainage contribute differently to the rising and recession limbs.

Apply it to interpretation

With similar runoff volume but an early peak, inspect routing. With overly rapid post-event recession, inspect soil and deeper storage contributions rather than treating both as the same parameter error.

Checks when interpreting results

Interpret storage changes together with routing; runoff generated in a cell is not the same as discharge at a downstream gauge.

Further explanation

Optional surface depression storage temporarily holds infiltration excess and accounts for evaporation and release. When enabled, distinguish generated excess rainfall from immediately routed surface runoff.

Continuous / long-term simulation

When simulating across rainfall events and seasons

What you gain

Examine how preceding rain and dry periods affect the next event response.

How it works · figure guide · checks

Reading the illustration

Notice how storage carries between rainy and dry periods.

Inputs and computation

  1. Input / state — prior state
  2. Processing — continuous update
  3. Output / use — next state

How it works and its scope

Storage is inherited from the preceding time step rather than reset for each event. Antecedent wetness and the initialization method therefore define the starting conditions.

Understand the mechanism

Carried storage lets dry-period evaporation and drainage alter the initial conditions of later storms. Continuous simulation therefore supports seasonal water availability and low-flow persistence as well as individual floods.

Apply it to interpretation

An accurate annual total can hide offsetting summer and winter errors. Monthly storage changes and dry-season flows help reveal this compensation.

Checks when interpreting results

Check continuous forcing and initial states, then review seasonal balance and recession behavior together.

Development and availability

Continuous and long-term simulation is established through public long-term watershed-runoff applications.

Evapotranspiration & soil water

When examining soil moisture between rainfall events

What you gain

Examine whether dry-period soil-water depletion changes the response to later rainfall.

How it works · figure guide · checks

Reading the illustration

Follow stored soil water returning to the atmosphere through vegetation and the surface. Image labels are in Korean.

Inputs and computation

  1. Input / state — weather + soil
  2. Processing — ET partition
  3. Output / use — storage change

How it works and its scope

Reference demand is allocated using root depth and layer overlap, then limited by water available in storage. Vegetation conditions, including land cover and seasonal LAI, also contribute.

Understand the mechanism

Meteorological evaporative demand differs from actual water loss. Vegetation and root overlap locate the supplying layers, while available canopy and soil storage limit supply.

Apply it to interpretation

Low actual evapotranspiration in hot weather can reflect soil-water limitation. Persistent overestimation can leave soil too dry before the next storm and alter runoff.

Checks when interpreting results

Review meteorological, soil and vegetation conditions, including the relationship between evaporative demand and stored water.

Further explanation

Canopy interception stores part of the rain on vegetation. After canopy evaporation, remaining demand passes to the soil. Unintercepted rain and canopy excess reach the ground; delayed canopy drainage is a separate option.

Snow accumulation & melt

When examining runoff during snow accumulation and melt

What you gain

Investigate whether snow accumulation and melt explain a delay between precipitation and runoff.

How it works · figure guide · checks

Reading the illustration

Follow rain/snow partitioning, melt-factor adjustment, snow storage and meltwater. Terrain conditions modify the melt factor.

Inputs and computation

  1. Input / state — precipitation + temperature
  2. Processing — rain/snow partition + melt
  3. Output / use — storage update + hydrologic input

How it works and its scope

Temperature partitions rain and snow. Elevation-based temperature adjustment and empirical season, slope, D8-direction and time factors modify melt; meltwater leaves snow storage and enters hydrologic inputs.

Understand the mechanism

Snow introduces seasonal storage between precipitation and runoff. Rain/snow partition and melt rate together explain winter storage and spring flow timing; empirical melt adjustment differs from a heat-budget calculation.

Apply it to interpretation

For an early spring rise, compare elevation-adjusted temperature, phase partition and melt timing before changing routing alone. Matching discharge can conceal incorrect snow storage.

Checks when interpreting results

Review precipitation, temperature, wind, elevation, slope and D8 direction. Direction/time adjustment is empirical, not a direct radiation or snow-transport calculation.

Development and availability

Snow accumulation and melt are established through public long-term snowmelt applications.

Separate hillslope & channel slopes

When representing different hillslope and channel slopes

What you gain

Identify whether hillslope or channel slope is relevant to a routing difference.

How it works · figure guide · checks

Reading the illustration

Identify the hillslope and channel pathways; the image is not a numerical comparison of slopes.

Inputs and computation

  1. Input / state — hillslope and channel slopes
  2. Processing — slope application by flow path
  3. Output / use — hillslope and channel routing

How it works and its scope

The slopes represent terrain conditions in separate domains, not successive processing stages. Each value is applied to its corresponding hillslope or channel calculation.

Understand the mechanism

Hillslope and channel slopes represent different flow paths. Separating them helps avoid routing a channel unrealistically fast by assigning a steep surrounding hillslope gradient.

Apply it to interpretation

For a timing mismatch, compare DEM hillslope gradients with the channel longitudinal profile. Changing both slope and roughness at once makes their effects harder to distinguish.

The relation between sediment supply and transport capacity governs erosion, movement and deposition. Interpret soil conditions, slope and velocity rather than using runoff volume as a proxy for sediment load.

Understand the mechanism

Sediment has both a soil supply and a flow transport capacity. Even with ample supply, reduced capacity can cause deposition, so sediment load is not a fixed multiple of runoff.

Apply it to interpretation

Discharge and sediment peaks can occur at different times in one storm. Review sources, soil conditions and hillslope connectivity, with separate comparison against sediment observations.

Checks when interpreting results

Review sediment sources, soil conditions and transport-capacity assumptions against sediment-specific evidence.

Development and availability

The hillslope sediment and erosion-deposition path is a research function still requiring dedicated public validation documentation.

2012

Flood-runoff simulation using radar rainfall

Watershed flood-runoff simulation with rainfall varying across space and time

The study examined radar-driven flood-runoff simulation for typhoon and convective storms in Korea's Namgang watershed.

Radar rainfall was mapped to computational grids, with hydrologic inputs derived from DEM, land-cover and soil data. Infiltration and surface/subsurface runoff were represented, and flow was routed through the channel network using kinematic waves.

This is an offline storm-event application published in 2012. It does not establish validation of the dynamic-wave river-network or 2D floodplain extensions now under development.

Long-term runoff simulation with mountain snow accumulation and melt

Long-term runoff simulation with watershed snow accumulation and melt

The application to Pakistan's Kunhar River Basin considered elevation-dependent weather, snow accumulation and melt in long-term runoff simulation.

GIS spatial inputs and elevation-dependent temperature and rainfall inputs were used to calculate long-term discharge with snow accumulation and melt.

This 2013 study concerns one mountain basin. Snow processes are supported by the public research record; application elsewhere still requires suitable weather inputs and basin-specific validation.

2017

Coupling rainfall runoff and groundwater flow

Coupled analysis of river flow continuing after rainfall ends

A 2017 conference presentation proposed coupling K-DRUM with MODFLOW for long-term water-cycle and low-flow analysis.

The proposed exchange passes K-DRUM deep percolation to MODFLOW as groundwater recharge, then returns MODFLOW baseflow to K-DRUM river runoff.

The record presents a coupling method and intended future use. It does not establish a measured accuracy improvement or coupling availability in the downloadable version. Current D-layer/baseflow-return and river-infiltration extensions remain ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT.
