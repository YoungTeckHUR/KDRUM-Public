K-DRUM

K-water Grid-based Distributed Rainfall rUnoff Model

A physically based, grid-distributed hydrologic model evolving into an integrated platform that connects watershed hydrology, river hydraulics and floodplain analysis.

The current v3.x line builds on public research in flood runoff, long-term hydrology, snowmelt, drought, parallel computation and surface-water–groundwater studies.

Explore the modelView research record

Four defining characteristics

A concise summary of K-DRUM's technical identity supported by the public research record.

Spatially distributedWatershed response is represented by grid-scale spatial variability rather than a single lumped unit.
Physically basedInfiltration, runoff, routing and water-balance processes are organized around physical concepts and equations.
Event to long-term hydrologyApplications extend from flood events to long-term runoff, snowmelt, drought and water-cycle studies.
Hydrology to hydraulicsThe v3.x line extends the established hydrologic core toward river and floodplain analysis.

Development maturity is stated explicitly

Established hydrologic functions and newer hydraulic/flood components are not presented at the same maturity level.

ESTABLISHED

Distributed hydrologic core

Grid rainfall-runoff, spatial rainfall, Green-Ampt infiltration, surface/subsurface runoff, kinematic routing, long-term hydrology, snow applications and an MPI research lineage are supported by the public record.

ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT

River hydraulics and 2D inundation

Cross-section-based 1D dynamic-wave networks, hydraulic structures, 2D inundation and integrated hydraulic outputs remain under continuing QA and validation.

VALIDATED DEVELOPMENT

Bidirectional 1D-2D exchange

Controlled development validation demonstrated active two-way exchange and internally consistent exchange accounting in the tested configuration.

RELEASE CANDIDATE

FloodViewer

The integrated result viewer is a release candidate, not a final public 1.0 product.

Current platform components

The calculation engine, project authoring, result interpretation and research extensions have distinct roles.

K-DRUM Core

The central engine for distributed hydrology and current hydrologic-hydraulic computation.

InputStudio

Project configuration, time series, geometry, scenarios, consistency checks and engine-input generation.

FloodViewer

Integrated interpretation of spatial and time-varying hydrologic and hydraulic results.

ChannelBed

Reusable high-resolution terrain, channel geometry and virtual channel-bed processing.

Integrated outputs

NetCDF-centered result exchange between the engine and Viewer.

Estuary2DV

Independent experimental prototype for longitudinal-vertical estuary hydrodynamics and salinity.
